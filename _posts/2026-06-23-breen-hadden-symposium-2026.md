PhD student Ronak Naeemaee recently presented her research at the Sheffield Hallam University Breen Hadden Symposium 2026, where she gave a talk entitled *Beyond Binary Splits in Survival Analysis: Time to Cut it Out*. The symposium brings together researchers from across the University's biomolecular sciences, engineering, and computing communities to showcase emerging research and foster interdisciplinary collaboration.

Ronak's presentation highlighted her doctoral research, which is developing new statistical approaches for validating cancer biomarkers. The work addresses longstanding limitations of conventional survival analysis by moving beyond arbitrary patient stratification methods and instead modelling continuous biomarkers alongside important clinical and demographic variables. By evaluating both the number and magnitude of significant stratification points across the full biomarker distribution, the framework enables identification of clinically actionable optimum cut points and provides informative features for downstream predictive modelling. This approach aims to improve the reproducibility and clinical relevance of biomarker validation while supporting more reliable precision oncology research.

As part of the project, Ronak is also developing harmonised tumour compendia spanning more than 800 open-access datasets across breast, lung, prostate, and colorectal cancers. Together with new open-source analytical tools, these resources will provide a robust framework for reproducible biomarker discovery and validation that is accessible to the wider research community.

The symposium provided an excellent opportunity to share ongoing work, receive valuable feedback from colleagues across disciplines, and strengthen collaborations as the project continues to develop.
