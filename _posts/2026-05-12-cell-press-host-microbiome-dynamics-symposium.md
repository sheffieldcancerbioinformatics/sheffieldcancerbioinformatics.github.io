We recently attended the Cell Press *Host-Microbiome Dynamics Symposium* in Bruges (May 10 - 12, 2026), which brought together a broad range of perspectives on host–microbe interactions, spanning fundamental microbial ecology through to translational and disease-focused research.

At the meeting, my collegaue Nick Peake of <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/shebcor" target="_blank">Sheffield Bowel Cancer Research Group (SHEBCOR)</a> and I presented ongoing collaborative work exploring extracellular vesicle (EV)-microbiome interactions in cancer. This included new results on *Escherichia coli* behavioural responses to tumour-derived EVs, highlighting disruptions to metal ion homeostasis and broader shifts in bacterial activity.

A central component of this work is our cross-species bioinformatics pipeline, *crossmirna*, which enables the systematic interrogation of EV-associated miRNA interactions with bacterial transcriptomes. The pipeline, developed within the group and accepted as an <a href="https://nf-co.re/" target="_blank">nf-core</a> community project in November 2025, provides a scalable and reproducible framework for analysing host-microbe regulatory interactions.

Using this approach, we identified candidate EV miRNA-*E. coli* transcriptome interactions that may contribute to observed phenotypic changes, offering a potential mechanistic link between tumour-derived vesicles and microbiome modulation.

The symposium provided a valuable opportunity to discuss these findings with researchers working across complementary experimental and computational domains, and to situate this work within the rapidly evolving landscape of microbiome research.

We look forward to developing these collaborations and continuing to refine analytical approaches for studying cross-kingdom communication in cancer systems.
