We are pleased to announce that from 2<sup>nd</sup> November 2026, Sheffield Cancer Bioinformatics Group will be moving to the University of Sheffield, where I will take up a new position as Lecturer in Bioscience.

This move represents a return to the University where I completed my PhD and spent much of my early research career. I originally trained as an experimental cancer biologist in the Bone Oncology group of Prof. Ingunn Holen and Prof. Penelope Ottewell, before moving increasingly towards computational cancer biology during my postdoctoral research. I subsequently worked as a cancer bioinformatician within the Sheffield Bioinformatics Core Facility and have continued to collaborate with colleagues at the University throughout my time at Sheffield Hallam University.

Since joining Sheffield Hallam in 2023, I have had the opportunity to develop an independent research programme bringing together cancer biology, bioinformatics and data science. Sheffield Cancer Bioinformatics has grown from this work, with our research focusing on understanding the mechanisms that enable cancer cells to survive treatment, persist in a dormant state and ultimately contribute to metastatic relapse.

From November, the group will continue this work at the University of Sheffield, combining experimental and computational approaches to investigate cancer progression, metastatic dormancy and therapeutic resistance. Our research will continue to integrate multi-omic profiling, bioinformatics, statistical modelling and machine learning, with the aim of identifying biomarkers and therapeutic vulnerabilities that can support precision oncology.

The move also provides an exciting opportunity to strengthen existing collaborations across cancer biology, bioinformatics and translational research in Sheffield, while continuing to develop new projects at the interface of experimental and computational biology.

I am very much looking forward to returning to the University of Sheffield and to the next chapter for Sheffield Cancer Bioinformatics.
